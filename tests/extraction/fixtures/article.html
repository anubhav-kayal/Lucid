How a small garden changes a city

Community gardens do more than add color to a neighborhood. They give people a shared place to grow food, meet neighbors, and learn how local ecosystems work. A single garden can turn an unused corner into a useful public space.

Researchers have found that regular access to green space can support daily movement and reduce the feeling that a neighborhood is unsafe. Gardeners also share tools and harvests, which makes small projects practical for people who do not have a yard of their own.

The strongest projects are designed with the people who will use them. Residents decide what to grow, how to manage water, and when the space should be open. Those decisions create habits that last longer than a one-time beautification effort.
